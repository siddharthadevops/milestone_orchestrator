# Goal: Machinery Proportionate to Risk

## Outcome

Milestone Orchestrator makes one evidence-backed proportionality decision for
each coherent change that introduces or materially changes machinery, or
explicitly reopens a machinery decision. This applies during design,
re-documentation, Brainstorming, implementation, fixing, and independent
review.

The decision selects the simplest sufficient response to the real authorised
need. It examines both the cost of building machinery and the plausible harm
of omitting it, so proportionality can remove unjustified mechanisms without
weakening real requirements or hiding valid defects.

## The machinery decision

The account for the change answers:

1. Who or what is affected without the change, what harm occurs, how exposed
   it is, and what authority establishes the need.
2. Which existing code, contracts, dependencies, and approved platform
   capabilities were inspected, and what can be reused or extended.
3. What the cheapest sufficient option is—including documentation,
   configuration, or no change—and why anything cheaper is insufficient.
4. What machinery remains to add, what authorised outcome it serves, and who
   will consume or observe it.
5. What it costs to build, migrate, operate, maintain, and review, considered
   alongside the cost and reversibility of omission.

If no machinery is added or changed, the account names the relevant surfaces
that were checked and says so.

An independently authoritative requirement fixes the outcome to satisfy;
proportionality chooses its simplest sufficient realization. A guarantee
created only by the working artifact, or made stricter than its governing
authority requires, is removed or weakened instead of authorising machinery.
If an authoritative outcome cannot be enforced, the result is a design gap
rather than a promise.

## How the decision travels

Use the existing workflow surfaces for the account:

- The skeleton and slice documents carry it through the current Question
  Battery and `Reuse Posture`, with each slice accounting only for its local
  machinery.
- A re-documentation wave treats the reopened skeleton and notes as one
  coherent change, receives the same proportionality and reuse context as an
  initial draft, produces one complete account for the set, and updates local
  postures where their machinery changed.
- Implementation and code fixes receive the governing account and add a
  compact account only for machinery introduced or changed by their delta.
  That account reaches the immediate independent review of the same candidate.
- A resumed rethink keeps the same obligation as the act that opened it.
- Review, delta review, and seal verify the account against the candidate and
  may challenge either disproportionate machinery or a harmful omission.
  Later reviews judge the resulting candidate without demanding another
  account for unchanged work.
- Brainstorming applies the check before proposing or accepting machinery. Its
  closing account is shown to every voter before the voter accepts or objects.

The account is evidence for judgment, not new design authority. Milestone-run
behavior follows the existing reform scope; standalone Brainstorming applies
the common check to every session.

## Completion

The milestone is complete when a coherent change has one reviewable machinery
decision, re-documentation does not multiply it per document, machinery added
during implementation or fixing reaches its immediate reviewer, and
Brainstorming voters decide with the complete account in view.
